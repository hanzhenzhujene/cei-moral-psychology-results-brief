Poster 1 · relevant benchmarks

Four papers, four different measurement targets

All four primary papers were obtained and reviewed. The original onboarding table flattened their methods into misleading metric labels.

Paper What the paper asks What our current run can answer Match Research lead interpretation
MoralBench Do model choices track human ratings, and can a model choose the statement people rated higher? Fresh agreement and forced-choice results, with saved intervals, under a different administration. Approximate Same task family, different scale and administration. Do not compare values numerically.
UniMoral Can a model predict actions, moral frameworks, influencing factors, and plausible consequences? Do cues change the result? No-cue label accuracy for three tasks and consequence METEOR. It cannot answer the cue question. Approximate The local run does not test the paper's cue effect, personalized prediction, or language ordering.
MoReBench Does the reasoning process cover expert-defined considerations, trade-offs, logic, and outcomes? Only whether four generic reasoning-word groups appear. Proxy only Local values detect generic reasoning words. They are not MoReBench scores.
MoralLens Which rationales appear, and do they change when reasoning comes before versus after the decision? Only keyword detectability and a binary expected-direction check. Proxy only The local adapter cannot establish an ethical theory or reproduce the paper's order effect.
Shared implication These benchmarks describe different coordinates: preference agreement, label prediction, procedural rubric fulfillment, and rationale classification. They must remain separate panels, not one “moral competence” score.
